ROAS in plain English

These are the numbers you should know before you scale an e-commerce campaign.

ROAS
Revenue / Ad Spend
A 2.0x ROAS means every $1 in ads produced $2 in attributed revenue. It does not automatically mean you made money.
Break-even ROAS
1 / Contribution Margin
The ROAS where the money left after product costs exactly covers ad spend. Higher is profitable; lower loses money on the first order.
CPA
Ad Spend / Orders
Cost per acquisition. Your break-even CPA is the contribution dollars available before ads on one average order.
Target ROAS
1 / (Margin - Profit Goal)
A safer scaling target than break-even because it leaves room for the profit margin you actually want to keep.
Why can a campaign have a good ROAS and still lose money?

Because ROAS only compares revenue with ad spend. It ignores product cost, shipping, fulfillment, payment fees, refunds, and other variable costs. A store with thin margins needs a much higher ROAS than a store with very high margins.

Should I optimize for break-even ROAS?

Usually no. Break-even is the floor, not the goal. If your campaign sits exactly at break-even, there is no first-order profit left for fixed overhead, payroll, software, taxes, or unexpected costs. Use the target ROAS output when you want a real profit buffer.

What is MER and how is it different from ROAS?

ROAS normally uses attributed ad revenue. MER (marketing efficiency ratio) uses total store revenue divided by total ad spend. MER is useful for looking at the whole business when attribution is imperfect, while campaign ROAS is useful for comparing individual paid campaigns.

Decision-support tool only. Taxes and fixed overhead are not included. For the best result, use your real blended AOV and variable costs from your store data.
